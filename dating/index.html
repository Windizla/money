Where furries actually meet each other

Let's be honest: if you search for "furry dating site," you'll mostly find a graveyard of abandoned projects, a couple of low-quality apps, and one or two places where the community genuinely gathers. The best connections happen in the spaces furries already use for everything else — art, gaming, fursuiting, con talk, and just hanging out.

That's actually good news. It means you get to meet people as whole people, not just as dating-profile thumbnails. Here's where to start:

  • Discord servers — The single biggest place furries hang out daily. There are general furry servers, regional groups, species-specific servers, LGBTQ+ furry spaces, fursuit maker discords, and dating/meeting channels on many of them.
  • Telegram groups — Especially popular in Eastern Europe and parts of Asia. Regional meetup groups are often organized via Telegram.
  • Conventions and local meetups — Nothing beats meeting IRL. Furry conventions and local furmeets are where friendships (and relationships) actually start.
  • Art and gallery sites — FurAffinity, Weasyl, Itaku, and similar. Many people meet through art commissions, collabs, and comment threads.
  • Mainstream dating apps — Tinder, Bumble, OkCupid, HER, and Grindr all have furry users. Put "furry" in your bio and tag it; people will find you.
  • Dedicated furry platforms — There are a few, but quality varies widely. We compare them below.

How to write a profile that works

Whether you're on Discord, a dating app, or FurAffinity, your profile is the first impression. A good profile doesn't sell you — it lets people know who you actually are. Here's what works:

Do

  • Lead with your interests, not your fursona. "Fursona: blue wolf" tells people nothing about you. "I do amateur photography, love rhythm games, and my blue wolf fursona has been with me since 2019" tells them a lot.
  • Have at least one picture that shows your actual build/face (blurred, masked, or partial is fine). Profiles with no IRL indicator feel like bots.
  • Be clear about what you're looking for. Friends? Relationship? Casual? Just con buddies? Saying "up for anything" reads as "I haven't thought about it."
  • Mention what you make or do. Do you draw? Fursuit? Write? Go to cons? Play an instrument? People connect over verbs, not adjectives.
  • Be upfront about being a furry if you're on a mainstream app. It filters out people who will be weird about it later.

Don't

  • Lead with "just ask" — it puts all the work on the other person.
  • Use only fursuit photos with zero IRL context (if dating/IRL is your goal).
  • Open with sexual content unless you're explicitly on a platform for that.
  • List fandom drama or "DNI" rants in your bio. It signals exhaustion, not boundaries.

Meeting IRL safely

So you've been chatting with someone online and you want to meet up — at a con, a furmeet, or a coffee. Great! IRL is where fandom friendships become real. Here's how to do it well:

  1. First meetup: public place, daytime. A coffee shop, a mall, a busy fursuit parade. Not someone's home, not a hotel room.
  2. Tell a friend where you're going and who you're meeting. Share their username/profile if possible.
  3. Have your own transport home, or at least a backup plan (rideshare, transit, a friend on call).
  4. At a con, use the hotel lobby or con space. Don't go to a stranger's room. Trust is built in public over time.
  5. Set your own pace. If someone pushes you to meet faster than you're comfortable with, that's a signal. Slow down or step away.

Online dating safety

We have a full guide on online dating safety for furries, but the essentials are:

⚠️ Safety basics
  • Never send money to someone you haven't met.
  • Video chat before meeting IRL if possible.
  • Watch for love-bombing, rushed intimacy, and "emergency" sob stories — they are the #1 scam pattern.
  • Don't share revealing photos with people you don't trust; they can be used for blackmail.
  • If a conversation makes you uncomfortable, leave. You don't owe anyone politeness at your own expense.

First date ideas in the fandom

Skip the generic dinner. Fandom first dates are more fun when they play to shared interests:

  • A local furmeet or mini-meetup — low-pressure, people around, instant shared context.
  • A con day together (if you're already both going) — panels, dealer's den, fursuit parade.
  • An art jam or craft night — bring sketchbooks, draw each other's sonas.
  • A zoo or wildlife park — oddly perfect for animal-people.
  • A board-game café with a couple of mutual friends — takes the pressure off.
  • A walk in a park with boba or coffee — simple, cheap, easy to extend or end.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a dedicated dating app for furries?

There is no single mainstream furry dating app. Most furries meet through fandom platforms (Discord, Telegram, FurAffinity), general dating apps with fandom tags, and in-person meets. Dedicated furry services exist but vary widely in quality and safety.

Is it safe to date within the fandom?

Like any community, the fandom has great people and risks. Use standard online dating safety: verify identities, meet in public first, tell friends where you're going, and trust your instincts. See our full safety guide.

How do I tell someone I'm a furry?

You don't owe anyone an immediate reveal. When dating within the fandom it usually comes up naturally; outside the fandom, bring it up when it feels right, like any other hobby or identity.

Can I just make friends, not date?

Absolutely. Most furries are looking for friends first. The fandom is overwhelmingly social, and many of the strongest connections are platonic. Start on community servers and at local meetups if that's your goal.

I'm new. Where do I even start?

Read our step-by-step guide for meeting furries, then pick one place to show up consistently — a server, a weekly art stream, or a local meet. Consistency beats signing up for ten platforms at once.
