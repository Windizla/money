A Short History of the Furry Fandom

The fandom grew from intersections of sci-fi, animation, comics, and early internet culture across forty years.

Roots (1960s–70s)

Anthropomorphic characters are ancient (myths, fables), and appear in Disney, Looney Tunes, Omaha the Cat Dancer, Cerebus, Usagi Yojimbo. Raw material, but not yet a fandom.

Formation (1980s)

Furry coalesced at sci-fi/comics cons where fans of anthropomorphic art found each other. APA fanzines like Rowrbrazzle let artists share work before the web.

Naming & first cons (late 80s–early 90s)

The term "furry" emerged. Confurence 0 (1989, Costa Mesa) was the first dedicated furry con.

Internet era (mid-90s–2000s)

Alt.fan.furry, VCL, Furcadia, deviantArt, then FurAffinity (2005) became public squares. Fursuit-making grew from home craft into an industry.

Mainstream awareness (2010s–present)

Social media, documentaries, COVID-era Twitch and streaming grew the fandom dramatically. It became more gender-diverse with stronger consent norms. Major cons now draw thousands.

Long-form history: The best English-language history remains Joe Strike's Furry Nation: The True Story of America's Most Misunderstood Subculture (2017, Cleis Press), with Fred Patten's essays and con-reports from the 1980s–2000s providing contemporary documentation of the fandom's emergence out of sci-fi, comics, and animation fandom. Patten's Funny Animals and More column and his co-founding of the first furry APA (Rowrbrazzle, 1989) are often cited as early markers of organized fandom..
